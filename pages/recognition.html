Pleasure vs. reward

In a neural network, a reward is a scalar numerical value (a positive or negative number) that tells the network how well it is doing. It is the primary feedback mechanism used in Reinforcement Learning to guide the network toward its goal

The drawbacks of this approach, in my opinion, are that it adds a separate scale in its own coordinate system, further complicating the entire approach to learning. This scale is also inherently dualistic, as rewards can be both positive and negative. As with any dualism, good and bad are unrelated, so the question always arises: To what extent should we consider this value to be strictly positive, and that to be strictly negative?

What I'm proposing is that danger and pleasure are a single data set. The value of the purpose is the amount of pleasure without which the goal cannot exist, just as pleasure cannot exist without the purpose. The amount of pleasure is the purpose. Thus, the single data set is an array of messages from all external and internal sensors. Each sensor has a specific sensitivity scale. A high level represents a constant state of alarm; the sensors detect the slightest deviation from the norm, both in their own state and in signals from external sensors. Danger signals prevent any activity from developing in the system. The appearance of the purpose begins to somehow disable or attenuate some of the alarm signals.
